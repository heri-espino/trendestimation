\section{Third-order PLS trend estimation and physical comparisons}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Observation model and third-order difference penalty}
Let $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_n)^\top$ be position measurements in
meters recorded at interval $\Delta t=0.1$\,s and let $\tau$
be their unknown smooth historical trajectory. The $(n-3)\times n$
difference matrix $D_3$ uses the stencil $(-1,3,-3,1)$;
$D_3\tau/(\Delta t)^3$ is a discrete jerk proxy in m/s$^3$.
Following \citet{guerrero2007}, we estimate the trajectory
by minimizing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pls}
\widehat\tau_{\lambda,\mu}=
\arg\min_{\tau\in\mathbb R^n}
\left\{\|y-\tau\|_2^2+
\lambda\|D_3\tau-\mu\mathbf1\|_2^2\right\},
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is the reference \emph{discrete} third difference,
and $\lambda\ge0$ controls regularization. Its physically
scaled reference jerk equals $\mu/(\Delta t)^3$.
Writing $Q=D_3^\top D_3$, the unique finite-$\lambda$
solution is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pls-solution}
\widehat\tau_{\lambda,\mu}
=(I+\lambda Q)^{-1}
(y+\lambda\mu D_3^\top\mathbf1).
\end{equation}

The $d=3$ polynomial nullspace has dimension three: unpenalized
quadratic position, linear velocity and constant acceleration.
At strong regularization, $\mu=0$ imposes a quadratic limit,
whereas fixed $\mu\ne0$ imposes a cubic position trajectory
with constant discrete third difference. These \emph{limits}
are not assumptions that real vehicles have constant jerk
throughout the observed interval.

\subsection{An interpretable, normalized smoothness index}
Let $\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_m>0$ be the positive eigenvalues
of $Q$, with $m=n-3$. The smoothness index used throughout the
\texttt{trendestimation} library is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S}
S(\lambda)=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^{m}
\frac{\lambda\delta_j}{1+\lambda\delta_j}
\in[0,1].
\end{equation}
It is monotone in $\lambda$ and therefore provides an
interpretable, comparable normalized regularization coordinate.
For fixed $\mu$, if $H_\lambda=(I+\lambda Q)^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edf}
\operatorname{df}(\lambda)=\operatorname{tr}(H_\lambda)
=n-mS(\lambda).
\end{equation}
This \emph{conditional} degrees-of-freedom formula must not
be applied unchanged when $\mu$ is estimated from the same
observations. Under the independent Gaussian variance-ratio
model, the smoothness index also admits the relative-noise
interpretation tested in Section~\ref{sec:precision};
it is not the absolute measurement precision of a sensor.

\subsection{Smoothing, reference jerk and choice of regularization}
We compare the existing PLS policies: a fixed reference
$\mu=0$; the sample mean of the noisy third differences;
a global cubic polynomial fitted to \emph{historical}
positions (its discrete third difference gives $\mu$);
and a jointly estimated reference from Gaussian restricted
likelihood. For the stochastic interpretation,
$\lambda=\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\sigma_u^2$ is the ratio
of observation noise variance to genuine discrete
third-difference innovation variance. The Gaussian
interpretation adds distributional assumptions beyond
the second-moment PLS construction of \citet{guerrero2007}.
When $\mu$ is estimated rather than fixed, the
mean-adjusted restricted likelihood must also eliminate
its nuisance mean direction.

We retain the library's GCV and Gaussian profile-ML choices
and explicitly separate \emph{fixed-$\mu$ profile likelihood}
from \emph{joint unknown-$\mu$ REML}; they need not minimize
the same physical loss. The original historical case
also screened LOOCV, AICc and restricted residual BIC,
as shown in its archived selection comparison. Here
they are background sensitivity analyses, not a paper
about computational methods or root-finding performance.
All tuning uses the training portion, never the held-out future.

\subsection{Truncated SVD as an \emph{estimator} baseline}
A full singular-value decomposition used solely to evaluate
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pls-solution} is a numerical implementation
of \emph{the same PLS estimator}, not a competitor. For a
meaningful comparison we instead use \emph{hard spectral
truncation} of the \emph{same} third-difference operator.
Write
\begin{equation}
D_3=U\,\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_m)V_+^\top,
\qquad 0<\sigma_1\le\cdots\le\sigma_m,
\end{equation}
where the $V_+$ columns are ordered from lowest to
highest difference roughness, and let $V_0$ span
$\ker D_3$. Choose $q$ satisfying
$D_3q=\mu\mathbf1$, for example
$q_i=\mu\binom{i-1}{3}$. PLS may be written as a
\emph{soft spectral filter}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pls-filter}
\widehat\tau_{\rm PLS}
=q+V_0V_0^\top(y-q)
+\sum_{j=1}^{m}
\frac{v_jv_j^\top(y-q)}{1+\lambda\sigma_j^2}.
\end{equation}
The truncated-SVD comparison is instead the
\emph{hard cutoff}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tsvd-filter}
\widehat\tau_{{\rm TSVD},r}
=q+V_0V_0^\top(y-q)+
\sum_{j=1}^{r}v_jv_j^\top(y-q),
\quad 0\le r\le m.
\end{equation}
This is a genuine, distinct low-roughness spectral
smoother. We specify two training-only comparisons:
(i) choose $r$ by GCV over the noninterpolating ranks
$0,\ldots,m-1$; (ii) match the PLS effective complexity
with $r=\operatorname{round}(\operatorname{df}_{\rm PLS}-3)$.
The latter is a matched-complexity \emph{ablation}, not
an independently optimized SVD procedure. Both use the
same training-only global cubic reference $\mu$ as
the corresponding profile-ML PLS comparator.
Their normalized hard-filter index is
$S_{\mathrm{TSVD}}=1-r/m$.

\paragraph{Conditioning on the estimated reference.}
The implemented TSVD rank score uses the residual
from the training-estimated cubic $\widehat\mu$, but
its conventional GCV denominator $n-(3+r)=m-r$
treats that reference as fixed. It is therefore
\emph{conditional plug-in GCV}, not exact GCV
for the entire data-dependent fitting map.
In particular, retaining $r$ near $m$ leaves
few nominal residual degrees of freedom.

The same issue makes effective-complexity matching
\emph{nominal} rather than exact. Write
$\widehat\mu=a^\top y$, where $a$ is the linear
cubic-OLS reference functional, $q_\mu=\mu q_1$
with $D_3q_1=\mathbf1$, and let $P_r$ project
onto the $3+r$ retained TSVD directions.
The full linear fits have effective degrees
of freedom
\begin{align}
\operatorname{df}_{\rm PLS}^{\rm plug}
 &=\operatorname{tr}(H_\lambda)
  +a^\top(I-H_\lambda)q_1,\\
\operatorname{df}_{\rm TSVD}^{\rm plug}
 &=3+r+a^\top(I-P_r)q_1 .
\end{align}
The rank matching in Experiment008 equates
only the fixed-reference trace components;
it does not equate these full plug-in
degrees of freedom. Both reference
estimates are based on historical data only.
Classical truncated singular-value regularization has
a broader inverse-problem literature; we do not claim
the hard-cutoff construction is new
\citep{renaut2019tsvd}.

\subsection{Other physical jerk and trajectory baselines}
The baseline families are (i) natural cubic smoothing
splines with their second-derivative roughness penalty
chosen by training-only GCV, (ii) Savitzky--Golay local
polynomial smoothing (window 21, degree three),
(iii) ordinary local polynomial and constant-velocity
or constant-acceleration extrapolations, and
(iv) raw third differences as an intentionally noisy
derivative reference \citep{savitzky1964,ahnert2007,cao2021}.
A classical cubic spline penalizes the \emph{second}
derivative, whereas Eq.~\eqref{eq:pls} penalizes
\emph{deviations of the third difference from $\mu$};
these are different physical smoothness assumptions.
The comparison measures historical discrete jerk only
when a synthetic trajectory supplies known truth, and
future position when a chronological future is available.

\subsection{Forecast continuation is separate from historical smoothing}
Given a fitted historical trend, the primary $d=3$
continuation assumes constant future third difference
equal to the chosen reference $\mu$. At future
integer horizon $h$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:d3-recurrence}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\tau_N(h)
&=\widehat\tau_N+h\nabla\widehat\tau_N\\
&\quad+\binom{h+1}{2}\nabla^2\widehat\tau_N
+\binom{h+2}{3}\mu.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This is the algebraic $d=3$ extension of the
lower-order forecasting construction of
\citet{guerrero2007}, \emph{not a new
probabilistic prediction model}. A secondary
continuation fits a quadratic to the last 25
fitted positions after subtracting the polynomial
particular solution $q$, then adds the future $q$.
Both schemes use only historical data; neither can
anticipate future changes in driver behavior.
A separate spline baseline uses a quadratic Taylor
continuation from spline endpoint derivatives.
Horizon-specific observed-position RMSE is reported
in meters.

\subsection{Two complementary experimental cases}
\paragraph{Known-truth simulation.}
In the completed Experiment005, we generated $1{,}296$
confirmatory analytic trajectories spanning six jerk
regimes, three observation-noise levels and three error
structures, with 80 historical observations and 20
clean future observations at 10\,Hz. Historical jerk
is scored against the \emph{same discrete operator}
applied to the true clean positions, not against an
unmatched instantaneous derivative. Experiment006
compared fixed and estimated references and Gaussian
parameter selection on these preserved cases. The
new Experiment008 compares the PLS and TSVD filters
with splines and Savitzky--Golay on \emph{exactly
these archived simulations}, without regenerating
them or refitting the original PLS parameter search.
Experiment007 separately generated $1{,}944$ paired
noise--dynamics calibration conditions, based on
72 independent innovation parents; its known-covariance
analysis is described in Section~\ref{sec:precision}.

\paragraph{Real recorded trajectories.}
The Federal Highway Administration/USDOT NGSIM
dataset (DOI: 10.21949/1504477) supplies camera-derived
longitudinal vehicle position at 10\,Hz
\citep{fhwa2016}. Source \texttt{Local\_Y} values
are converted from feet to meters by $0.3048$.
The initial descriptive illustration used 15 overlapping
chronological windows from five US-101 track segments
that contain only two distinct integer vehicle IDs.
A larger fixed-source follow-up selected 200 quality-qualified
episodes (100 US-101 and 100 I-80); their IDs
cannot be equated with 200 unique physical vehicles.
Every episode supplies 80 historical positions and
withholds $h=5,10,20$ future frames at a prespecified
origin. The new TSVD comparison reuses the
\emph{same} archived 200 episodes and training-only
PLS parameter choices. Real jerk truth and sensor
precision are \emph{not independently observed}.
The 200 episodes represent five coarse recording-time
blocks, so pooled forecasting scores describe this
convenience sample, not independent vehicle-population
inference. Source queries, file checksums, configuration
and raw/fitted outcome rows remain documented separately
for every experiment.

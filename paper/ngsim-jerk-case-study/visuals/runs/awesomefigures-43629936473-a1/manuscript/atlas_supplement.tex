\section*{Scope and provenance}
This visual appendix collects the \emph{completed}, frozen numerical outcomes
from synthetic known-jerk Experiments005--008 and the real FHWA NGSIM
comparison; no forecasting model, SVD rank, smoothing parameter, or
stochastic trajectory has been recomputed. The full rendering code
uses the pinned \texttt{AwesomeFigures} library
(\texttt{set\_style}, \texttt{color\_palette},
\texttt{plot\_scheme}, \texttt{save\_figure}) and
publishes editable SVGs, PDF vectors, PNG previews,
the exact immutable source-archive SHA-256 hashes, and output inventories.
Each source and each figure is addressable in
\texttt{generated/awesomefigures\_atlas\_manifest.json}.

Synthetic reference jerk is the \emph{known discrete} third difference
of clean position divided by $(0.1\ \mathrm s)^3$, and each forecast
window's RMSE is cumulative over frames 1 through $H$ (not the
terminal frame). The real NGSIM positions are camera-derived recordings:
neither latent clean jerk nor sensor precision is directly observed.
The 200 NGSIM episodes share vehicles and recording-time blocks,
so site-level and episode-level analyses are descriptive.
Experiment007's 1,944 factorial outcomes share only
72 independently drawn parent innovation sequences.

\atlasfigure{A1. Known-jerk regimes}
{atlas_known_jerk_regimes}
{Logarithmic historical discrete-jerk RMSE for five \emph{distinct}
smoothers, by six prespecified synthetic jerk regimes.
Each regime averages the same 216 independent trajectory cases,
crossing three observation-noise levels, three error structures and
24 replicates.}
{A model's relative performance may change with genuine motion
dynamics. These are not real jerk observations.}

\atlasfigure{A2. Conditional-GCV rank instability}
{atlas_tsvd_rank_pathology}
{Distribution of the 1,296 chosen hard TSVD ranks and
cumulative squared historical jerk error by rank. Ranks 75--76
(out of 77 positive roughness modes) occur in 145 cases
and contribute 78.9\% of TSVD-GCV's total squared jerk error.}
{The archival position-oriented score is a conditional
plug-in GCV criterion, not exact full data-dependent GCV.
Near-interpolation is extremely costly for third derivatives.}

\atlasfigure{A3. Paired synthetic losses}
{atlas_paired_jerk_losses}
{Each point is one frozen simulated trajectory.
The axes show root historical discrete-jerk MSE for
PLS and nominal fixed-reference EDF-matched hard TSVD;
six panels separate different jerk regimes.}
{The diagonal indicates equal episode-level performance.
The 1,296 points are paired observations from the same
synthetic design, not independent estimates of real driver jerk.}

\atlasfigure{A4. Reconstruction and extrapolation are different targets}
{atlas_method_tradeoff}
{The same five historical smoothers plotted by
known synthetic jerk RMSE versus cumulative first-second
\emph{clean-position} forecast RMSE with the same
last-25-point quadratic continuation.}
{Low position prediction error does not imply low
third-derivative error. The x-axis is not an endpoint-only error.}

\atlasfigure{A5. NGSIM site heterogeneity}
{atlas_ngsim_by_site}
{The five historical smoother plus shared three-point continuation
procedures, evaluated separately on 100 selected episodes
from US-101 and 100 from I-80. Each curve is the
cumulative observed-position forecast RMSE through
0.5, 1, or 2 seconds.}
{Site-specific evaluations are descriptive, not replicated
population inference. The separate spline quadratic-Taylor rule
is excluded because only its pooled archived benchmark
is included in this frozen comparison.}

\atlasfigure{A6. Paired NGSIM episode variability}
{atlas_ngsim_paired_delta}
{Paired difference in episode-level \emph{first-second window MSE}
relative to PLS: nominally matched TSVD on the left, and
GCV smoothing spline under the same three-point continuation
on the right. Color distinguishes the two sites;
the signed-log transformation retains the direction of each difference.}
{Points from shared site/time blocks must not be treated as
independent physical vehicles. A negative ordinate means that
the comparator has lower recorded-position loss for that episode.}

\atlasfigure{A7. Correct-model smoothness calibration}
{atlas_spectral_calibration_grid}
{Selected normalized smoothness $S$ versus the known covariance
observation-noise fraction in nine independent-Gaussian
factorial cells, each averaged over 24 parent draws.
Four selectors appear separately, including the
oracle known-reference control.}
{The identity line has a conditional covariance-model
interpretation; $S$ is not a physical sensor-resolution scale.}

\atlasfigure{A8. Full calibration-misspecification matrix}
{atlas_misspecification_matrix}
{Mean absolute spectral-noise calibration error under
three jerk-innovation structures and three observation-error
structures, for all four archived smoothing selectors.}
{Serial measurement-error correlation or structural
changes in jerk invalidate the independent Gaussian
variance-ratio calibration. Cells share parent draws.}

\atlasfigure{A9. Two different noise interventions}
{atlas_noise_jerk_interventions}
{Joint unknown-mean REML's selected-$S$ changes under
increased observation noise (left) and increased genuine
jerk volatility (right), against known covariance effects.
Error bars are archived parent-level 95\% bootstrap intervals.}
{Parent-level dependence is respected. Raising genuine
jerk innovation volatility can \emph{decrease} selected $S$.}

\atlasfigure{A10. Reference-mean sensitivity}
{atlas_reference_policy}
{Historical known-discrete-jerk RMSE of five previously
completed Experiment006 penalization/reference-mean strategies
over the same 1,296 synthetic trajectories.}
{A nonzero cubic-OLS reference combined with profile likelihood
performed well in this tested mixture, but it is not an
universal likelihood or physical optimality result.}

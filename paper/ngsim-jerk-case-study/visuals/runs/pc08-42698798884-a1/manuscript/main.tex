\begin{abstract}
Third differences of recorded vehicle position provide a physically interpretable, but noise-sensitive, proxy for jerk. We study third-order penalized least-squares (PLS) trend estimation through the normalized smoothness index $S$, which controls the effective number of fitted degrees of freedom and, under a Gaussian variance-ratio model, represents the average spectral fraction of differenced variation attributable to observation error. We validate this interpretation in a paired factorial simulation with known clean positions and jerk: 1,944 confirmatory noise--dynamics conditions, 72 independent random-innovation parent draws, and four smoothing selectors. Under correctly specified independent Gaussian errors, joint mean-adjusted restricted likelihood yields correlation $0.965$ between selected and theoretical $S$, with mean absolute calibration error $0.0115$. Correlated observation errors increase this error substantially; and equal proportional scaling of observation noise and jerk innovations leaves $S$ unchanged despite different absolute sensor precision. A separate known-jerk simulation compares reference-mean policies and forecast continuations; no selector is uniformly best for both reconstruction and prediction. Finally, chronological extrapolation is assessed on camera-derived Federal Highway Administration NGSIM positions, including a 200-episode follow-up in which a GCV smoothing spline outperforms the investigated PLS forecasting variants in pooled one-second RMSE. We distinguish regularization, relative noise attribution, physical jerk accuracy, and future-position prediction. The contribution is a reproducible, model-conditional characterization of the index for vehicle kinematics, not a new PLS estimator or a universal sensor-quality score.
\end{abstract}

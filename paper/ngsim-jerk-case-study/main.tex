\shorttitle{Third-order PLS for jerk and vehicle trajectories}
\shortauthors{Espino-Montelongo and Cort\'es-Toto}
\title[mode=title]{Interpretable third-order penalized least-squares trend estimation: known-jerk simulations and real vehicle trajectories}
% Provisional author attribution, pending coauthor approval.
\author[1]{Heriberto Espino-Montelongo}
\author[1]{Daniela Cort\'es-Toto}
\affiliation[1]{organization={Universidad de las Am\'ericas Puebla},city={San Andr\'es Cholula},state={Puebla},country={Mexico}}
\begin{abstract}
The third difference of recorded vehicle position approximates jerk but strongly amplifies observation noise. We investigate the established third-order penalized least-squares (PLS) trend model, its reference third-difference mean and the normalized, interpretable smoothness index $S$, with a focus on \emph{physical reconstruction and forecasting}, not solver execution time. In the independent Gaussian variance-ratio model, $S$ equals the average spectral fraction of the observed third-difference covariance attributable to measurement noise. A controlled relative-noise calibration experiment with 1,944 paired noise--jerk conditions (72 independent innovation parents) finds Spearman association $0.965$ and mean absolute calibration error $0.0115$ for joint-REML-selected $S$ under correct covariance assumptions, while correlated observation noise substantially worsens calibration. We also compare PLS with hard truncated-SVD filtering of the same third-difference operator, GCV cubic smoothing splines and Savitzky--Golay filtering using 1,296 archived known-jerk synthetic trajectories. PLS with a historical cubic-OLS reference and Gaussian profile-likelihood selection attains historical discrete-jerk RMSE $2.441$\,m/s$^3$, versus $2.942$\,m/s$^3$ for SVD truncation matched in effective complexity. Real-data forecasting is assessed separately on 200 quality-qualified Federal Highway Administration NGSIM episodes, where the PLS and matched-SVD three-point continuations are comparable and a GCV smoothing spline with quadratic Taylor continuation achieves the smallest pooled recorded-position RMSE. Thus third-order PLS provides a physically interpretable, tunable estimator for noisy jerk, but $S$ is a \emph{relative, model-dependent} noise index rather than absolute sensor precision, and no estimator dominates every physical or forecasting target.
\end{abstract}

\title[mode=title]{Interpretable third-order penalized trend estimation for vehicle jerk: relative-noise calibration and forecasting}

\author[1]{Heriberto Espino-Montelongo}
\author[1]{Daniela Cort\'es-Toto}
\affiliation[1]{organization={Universidad de las Am\'ericas Puebla},city={San Andr\'es Cholula},state={Puebla},country={Mexico}}
\begin{abstract}
Estimating physical jerk from vehicle positions is difficult because third differences amplify observation noise. We investigate third-order penalized least-squares (PLS) trend estimation with a nonzero reference third difference and an interpretable normalized smoothness index $S$. We describe the relationship between $S$, effective degrees of freedom and, under an independent Gaussian variance-ratio model, relative spectral attribution of third-difference variability to observation noise. Two simulation studies supply known position and jerk: a 1,296-trajectory comparison of PLS smoothing choices with established spline and local-polynomial filters, and a 1,944-condition paired calibration study with 72 independent random-innovation parents. In the correctly specified Gaussian calibration setting, joint restricted-likelihood selection achieves Spearman association $0.965$ between estimated and theoretical $S$ (mean absolute error $0.0115$); serially correlated observation errors substantially degrade calibration. In the known-jerk comparison, a global cubic reference mean with profiled Gaussian likelihood performs well for jerk recovery, whereas no smoothing rule is uniformly best for subsequent position forecasts. We then evaluate recorded-future position predictions on 200 real NGSIM trajectory episodes and compare PLS with GCV smoothing splines, Savitzky--Golay and polynomial baselines; the spline has the lowest pooled forecast error at all evaluated horizons. Third-order PLS offers a physically meaningful smoothing coordinate, not a universally optimal forecast or an absolute measure of sensor precision.
\end{abstract}

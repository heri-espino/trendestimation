\section{Third-order penalized least-squares trend estimation}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Trend and reference third differences}
Let $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_n)^\top$ denote equally spaced
longitudinal positions sampled at interval $\Delta t$.
Write $\tau$ for the unobserved positional trend.
The order-$d$ difference matrix
$D_d\in\mathbb R^{(n-d)\times n}$ annihilates
polynomials of degree at most $d-1$. The penalized
least-squares (PLS) estimator of \citet{guerrero2007} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pls}
\widehat\tau_{\lambda,\mu}=
\arg\min_{\tau\in\mathbb R^n}
\left\{\|y-\tau\|_2^2+
\lambda\|D_d\tau-\mu\mathbf1\|_2^2\right\},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda\ge0$ controls regularization and $\mu$
is the reference mean of the $d$th differences.
For $Q_d=D_d^\top D_d$, differentiation gives the
unique solution at finite $\lambda$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pls-solution}
\widehat\tau_{\lambda,\mu}
=(I+\lambda Q_d)^{-1}
(y+\lambda\mu D_d^\top\mathbf1).
\end{equation}
The estimator is historical trend reconstruction;
forecasting requires an additional extension rule.

We focus on $d=3$ because
$D_3\tau_i/(\Delta t)^3$ is a discrete approximation
to physical jerk, the time derivative of acceleration.
When $\mu=0$, strong penalization favors a quadratic
position trend (approximately constant acceleration).
For fixed nonzero $\mu$, the limiting trend is cubic
with prescribed discrete third difference, representing
approximately constant nonzero jerk. These are
\emph{modeling assumptions}, not independent physical
measurements. Results for other difference orders
provide secondary comparisons, not the central claim.

\subsection{An interpretable smoothing coordinate}
Let $\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_{n-d}>0$ be the nonzero
eigenvalues of $Q_d$; the remaining $d$ eigenvalues
are zero because $\dim\ker D_d=d$. Define the
normalized smoothness index
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:S}
S_d(\lambda)=\frac{1}{n-d}
\sum_{j=1}^{n-d}
\frac{\lambda\delta_j}{1+\lambda\delta_j}
\in[0,1].
\end{equation}
For fixed $d$ and $n$, this is monotone in $\lambda$,
with $S_d(0)=0$ and $S_d(\infty)=1$.
Thus $S$ provides a comparable, bounded description
of imposed trend smoothness rather than requiring the
reader to interpret arbitrary penalty magnitudes.
For \emph{fixed} $\mu$, the smoothing matrix is
$H_\lambda=(I+\lambda Q_d)^{-1}$ and its effective
degrees of freedom satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:effective-df}
\operatorname{df}(H_\lambda)
=\operatorname{tr}(H_\lambda)
=n-(n-d)S_d(\lambda).
\end{equation}
For $d=3$, $n=80$, this varies from 80 to 3.
If $\mu$ is estimated from the same observations,
the effective complexity of the \emph{complete}
fitted estimator may be larger, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:effective-df} should not be interpreted
as its unconditional degrees of freedom.

The stochastic variance-ratio interpretation
of \citet{guerrero2007} and the percentage-smoothness
work of \citet{guerrero2008} motivate this index.
Our contribution is not to introduce PLS or the
original notion of smoothness, but to examine
$S$ for third-order vehicle kinematics with
known-truth calibration and observed trajectories.
Section~\ref{sec:precision} tests the stronger
\emph{model-conditional} spectral noise interpretation;
the index alone does not measure absolute sensor
precision.

\subsection{Selecting $\lambda$ and the reference $\mu$}
We distinguish the \emph{reference-mean policy} from
the \emph{smoothing criterion}. Reference means are
fixed $\mu=0$, the observed training mean
$\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{raw}}=\mathbf1^\top
D_3y/(n-3)$, and a cubic OLS reference
$\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{OLS}}
=6\widehat a_3(\Delta t)^3$ when
a cubic in physical time has coefficient
$\widehat a_3$. The raw difference mean is
sensitive to measurement errors at the boundary.
Our synthetic-only known-$\mu$ method is explicitly
an oracle \emph{control}, never a practical estimator
on real observations.

For the same fixed-$\mu$ PLS fit, historical training
observations select $\lambda$ through generalized
cross-validation (GCV) or a profiled Gaussian
variance-components likelihood. Classical ordinary
leave-one-out CV, AICc and a constrained residual BIC
are also described for the preliminary real pilot,
following \citet{cortes2017}; those pilot criteria
are not five independent pieces of forecast evidence.
In historical experiments the search is over
predeclared candidate $S$ values; the later
likelihood comparisons refine a continuous
one-dimensional search and explicitly check the
variance-ratio boundary limits. Grid and continuous
results are identified separately.

For the Gaussian model
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gaussian-model}
y=\tau+\varepsilon,\quad
D_3\tau=\mu\mathbf1+u,\quad
\varepsilon\sim N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2I),
\quad u\sim N(0,\sigma_u^2I),
\end{equation}
with independent disturbances, set
$B=D_3D_3^\top$, $m=n-3$ and
$W_\lambda=I_m+\lambda B$.
The variance ratio
$\lambda=\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\sigma_u^2$
defines the fixed-$\mu$ Gaussian restricted
likelihood of $D_3y-\mu\mathbf1$.
When $\mu$ is unknown, the generalized
least-squares mean at each penalty is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gls-mu}
\widehat\mu(\lambda)=
\frac{\mathbf1^\top W_\lambda^{-1}D_3y}
{\mathbf1^\top W_\lambda^{-1}\mathbf1}.
\end{equation}
Writing $r_\lambda=D_3y-
\widehat\mu(\lambda)\mathbf1$, the corresponding
mean-adjusted profiled restricted likelihood,
apart from penalty-independent terms, is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reml-mean}
\begin{aligned}
F_R(\lambda)
={}&\log|W_\lambda|
+\log(\mathbf1^\top W_\lambda^{-1}\mathbf1)\\
&+(m-1)\log\left(
\frac{r_\lambda^\top
W_\lambda^{-1}r_\lambda}{m-1}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This Gaussian extension to Guerrero's original
second-moment formulation is statistically distinct
from simply inserting a sample-derived $\mu$ in
the \emph{fixed}-mean likelihood. It does not
guarantee minimum physical jerk or forecast error.

\subsection{Conventional smoothers and comparable outcomes}
To evaluate PLS against established derivative
estimators, the simulation and real-data studies
include natural cubic smoothing splines with a
training-only GCV-selected second-derivative
penalty \citep{craven1979}, Savitzky--Golay local
polynomial filtering \citep{savitzky1964},
and local quadratic or cubic polynomial trends.
For prediction, simple constant-velocity and
constant-acceleration extrapolations provide
additional physical baselines. These methods
are distinguished from PLS by both their
regularization constraints and their extrapolation
rules; in particular, a natural cubic smoothing
spline penalizes a \emph{second} derivative,
whereas $D_3$ PLS penalizes discrete
\emph{third} differences. We do not assert
that one parameterization of smoothness makes
these estimators identical \citep{ahnert2007}.

The PLS historical fit is extrapolated either
by a local polynomial fitted to the final
25 smoothed observations or by the recurrence
obtained from the last three fitted values.
With $\nabla$ denoting a backward difference,
the latter is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:d3-recurrence}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\tau_N(h)
={}&\widehat\tau_N+h\nabla\widehat\tau_N
+\binom{h+1}{2}\nabla^2\widehat\tau_N\\
&+\binom{h+2}{3}\mu,\qquad h\ge1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
It is the direct $d=3$ algebraic extension
of the constant-difference extrapolation logic
discussed by \citet{guerrero2007} for lower
orders, \emph{not} a separate optimal
probabilistic forecast model.
Natural cubic smoothing-spline forecasts use
training-fitted endpoint derivatives or a
specified local polynomial continuation.
Every reported forecast is scored on
chronologically withheld future positions,
with no future observations used to choose
the smoothing or reference parameters.

We report jerk RMSE only where a synthetic
generating trajectory supplies the true
discrete third difference. On camera-derived
NGSIM positions, the target is held-out
\emph{recorded position}; physically true jerk
and observation error are unavailable.

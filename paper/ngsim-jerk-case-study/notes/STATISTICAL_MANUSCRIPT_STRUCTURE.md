# Paper C: statistical-methodology manuscript structure and results handoff

**Author decision:** This paper is about **third-order PLS trend estimation, its normalized interpretable smoothness index S, and comparisons with established smoothing methods**. It is **not a computational speed, matrix factorization, or linear algebra benchmark**. The computational benchmarking research remains intact and separate in the repository.

**Canonical source:** `paper/ngsim-jerk-case-study/main.tex` on branch `paper/paper-c-statistical-methods-simulation-real`, stacked on `paper/paper-c-visual-suite-exp007` (containing the source-derived AwesomeFigures visual suite). The compiled `main.pdf` is generated by the dedicated PCAG103A-08 workflow. The parent visual branch and all underlying numerical experiment results remain untouched.

## Exact manuscript order

1. `sections/introduction.tex`: physical derivative amplification, research objectives and distinct availability of known truth.
2. `sections/methods.tex`: Guerrero's PLS objective, third-difference interpretation, normalized S and degrees of freedom, reference \(\mu\), GCV/profile likelihood/unknown-mean REML, classical smoothing splines, Savitzky–Golay and local polynomials, forecast continuation.
3. `sections/simulation_benchmarks.tex`: **Simulation Study I**, the existing Experiment005 analytic known-jerk trajectories, the existing Experiment006 reanalysis using exactly the same trials, standard smoother comparisons and different targets (historical physical jerk versus future clean position).
4. `sections/precision_calibration.tex`: **Simulation Study II**, the independently created Experiment007 factorial calibration for S under known independent/correlated observation noise and known jerk innovation volatility. Physical covariance theory, paired interventions, calibration figures and failure under AR(1) errors.
5. `sections/real_data_study.tex`: **real NGSIM application**, the completed **200 episodes** from I-80 and US-101 in Experiment004, plus the matched Experiment006 joint-REML follow-up. Main table contrasts PLS, training-only GCV natural cubic smoothing spline, Savitzky–Golay and raw local quadratic. The original small five-segment pilot from Experiments001–003 is preserved only as an exploratory visual illustration.
6. `sections/discussion.tex`: model-dependent relative noise attribution versus absolute precision, synthetic known-truth and real recorded-future distinction, limits and data rights.

The old `sections/results.tex`, `generated/solver_table.tex`, and `figures/backend_comparison.pdf` **remain committed to the repository**, but are deliberately **not input into the compiled statistical manuscript**. This change does not erase any historical experiment or any original figure/data.

**Simulation I standard comparison (historical known jerk RMSE in m/s³; Experiment005):** PLS profile ML/cubic OLS reference 2.9177, PLS profile ML/zero reference 3.0207, Savitzky–Golay 36.8291, GCV cubic spline 60.9131, GCV PLS/zero mean 68.2422. These numbers are specific to the predeclared synthetic motion/noise regimes. The continuous-profile reanalysis (Experiment006) attains 2.4413 m/s³ for cubic OLS mu versus 3.2950 for unknown-mean joint REML; at one second, clean position RMSE with local25 is 0.4053 m for fixed-zero/continuous ML versus 0.4977 m for joint REML. Do not compare jerk and future-position RMSE as the same loss.

**Simulation II interpretation (Experiment007):** Under iid Gaussian jerk and iid Gaussian position errors, selected S under joint REML has descriptive Spearman 0.965 with the exact theoretical fraction and MAE 0.0115. Increasing measurement SD raises S; increasing actual jerk volatility lowers S. Equal proportionate scaling of both leaves S constant while observation variance changes by a factor of 16. Correlated observations produce substantial calibration MAE (0.0712 or 0.1111 in the tested AR(1) configurations). Therefore S is not a universal measurement-precision metric.

**Real main comparison (200 episodes; Experiment004):** GCV natural cubic spline/quadratic Taylor RMSE 0.1354/0.3480/0.8787 m at 0.5/1/2 seconds. PLS profile ML/zero mean/three-point recurrence 0.1433/0.5124/1.8686; raw quadratic 0.3406/0.6184/1.3447; Savitzky–Golay/quadratic Taylor 0.3547/0.8506/2.4106. The spline is the strongest pooled reported method for future recorded positions. The matched Experiment006 continuous unknown-mean REML selects S=0 in 161 of 200 real episodes. Do not claim PLS generally forecasts best or physically validates NGSIM jerk.

# Fan, Jin & Yin (2024) — Two-step quadratic programming for longitudinal vehicles

**Citation:** Ximeng Fan, Wen-Long Jin, Penghang Yin. *Transportation Science* 58(6):1371–1388. DOI: [10.1287/trsc.2024.0524](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.2024.0524); published online 22 August 2024.

**Inspected:** The author's supplied journal abstract, INFORMS publisher record, and publicly available [ISTTT25 proceedings version](https://limos.engin.umich.edu/isttt25/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/sites/4/2024/06/ISTTT25-proceedings-050-min.pdf). The journal main-body equations were not independently checked against the final typeset PDF.

## Problem and central idea

Noisy camera-derived longitudinal positions can imply implausible speed, acceleration and jerk after differentiation. The authors argue that unconstrained fidelity–roughness smoothing may fail to enforce realistic bounds and that data quality should respect both physical constraints and a prior estimate of measurement error.

They propose **two sequential convex quadratic programs**, rather than our single unconstrained penalized least-squares problem.

1. **Step 1 — fidelity first:** Find *half-smoothed positions* near the raw observations, subject to physically meaningful constraints on speeds and higher-order derivatives.
2. **Step 2 — smoothness second:** Minimize trajectory roughness while preserving derivative bounds and constraining the deviation from observed positions using the first-stage errors and prior position-error information.

The roughness quadratic in the second stage is not positive definite in the original variables. The authors add appropriate initial-position equalities, with count matching the highest derivative order, and prove existence and uniqueness for the resulting problems. The proceedings text explicitly distinguishes their approach from a one-stage weighted sum of residual and roughness losses.

## Experiment and reported evidence

- The main setting is longitudinal **NGSIM** trajectories. The publicly documented comparison also involves the **highD** dataset.
- The authors report favorable results when the maximum imposed derivative order is **three** (jerk) and compare with some manually re-extracted trajectories.
- The minimum-jerk principle is a motivating physical interpretation, not independent proof that any particular smoothed jerk curve is the latent physical jerk.

## Our reading annotations

- **Closest direct prior art.** It addresses almost the same *application*, including NGSIM, quadratic optimization and a third-derivative choice.
- **Different mathematical specification.** Our estimator minimizes
  $$\|y-\tau\|_2^2+\lambda\|D_3\tau-\mu\mathbf1\|_2^2.$$
  It is a single *soft* quadratic penalty centered on a reference third difference; Fan et al. instead use separate error-constrained and roughness-constrained stages. The available abstract does not establish their use of a **nonzero centered mean** inside our exact penalty.
- **Physical guarantees differ.** Their hard bounds can guarantee adherence to specified derivative ranges when the optimization is feasible. Our unconstrained PLS solution **does not** guarantee feasible speed, acceleration or jerk.
- **Do not claim** the first third-derivative smoother, first NGSIM quadratic-programming smoother, or first physically meaningful vehicle-trajectory smoothing method.
- **Baseline requirement:** compare both with common raw positions, time sampling, postprocessing and numerical error metrics. If using the published algorithm, document which parts of its position-error and bound priors can be reproduced.

## Useful manuscript placement

Introduction: leading close predecessor immediately after earlier NGSIM filtering papers. Discussion: explicitly contrast *soft reference-jerk shrinkage* with *hard derivative constraints*, and acknowledge that our current five-vehicle pilot is much narrower than their validation.

**Open check:** read the final article's full methods/supplement for any reference-mean variant and detail its data split and physical bounds before wording an exclusive priority claim.

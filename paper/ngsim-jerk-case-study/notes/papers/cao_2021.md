# Cao et al. (2021) — V-Spline: adaptive smoothing with velocity observations

**Citation:** Zhanglong Cao, David Bryant, Timothy C. A. Molteno, Colin Fox and Matthew Parry. “V-Spline: An Adaptive Smoothing Spline for Trajectory Reconstruction.” *Sensors* 21(9):3215 (2021), [DOI 10.3390/s21093215](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21093215).

**Inspected primary source:** User-supplied 16-page published *Sensors* PDF, including mathematical formulation, cross-validation, simulations and a tractor GPS application.

## Research problem

The goal is a reconstructed continuous trajectory from **noisy measured positions and separately observed velocities**, potentially at irregular time points. This is a distinct data situation from our NGSIM study, which intentionally uses recorded longitudinal **positions alone** as estimator inputs.

## Objective and central methods

Their continuous-time estimator minimizes, in a simplified notation,

$$
J[f]=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i-f(t_i))^2
+\frac{\gamma}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n(v_i-f'(t_i))^2
+\int_a^b\lambda(t)\{f''(t)\}^2\,dt.
$$

Here $\gamma>0$ weights the independent velocity data and $\lambda(t)$ controls the **second derivative (acceleration)**. The model uses piecewise cubic Hermite spline bases and produces a linear smoother in the position and velocity observations. See PDF eqs. (2), (10)–(15), pp.3–5.

- A theorem states uniqueness of the minimizing V-spline (p.3).
- The **adaptive** version changes the interval penalty using observed time gaps and average speeds; a recommended form is $\lambda_i=\eta\Delta T_i/\bar v_i^2$, where numerical handling near $\bar v_i=0$ deserves care (p.5, eq. 18).
- Cross-validation jointly chooses the spline and velocity weighting parameters. The article derives a leave-one-out shortcut from smoother matrices (pp.6–7, eqs. 20–22). This is not a forecasting-horizon criterion.

## Evidence and limits

- Synthetic tests use integrated Blocks, Bumps, HeaviSine and Doppler velocity signals with added position and velocity noise, comparing reconstructed **true positions** under known simulation truth (pp.7–9).
- The empirical example is a **two-dimensional tractor GPS trajectory** with 928 measurements, speed/bearing and operating status; not NGSIM. The penalty can depend on whether the tractor boom is raised or lowered (pp.9–10).
- In the authors' simulations, the adaptive and non-adaptive V-splines perform strongly against the listed alternatives. Their result does not transfer automatically to jerk RMSE or NGSIM forecasting.

## Annotations for our Paper C

- **Related PLS framework:** both are quadratic regularized linear smoothers with cross-validation, but continuous-time acceleration roughness differs from discrete third-difference reference-jerk shrinkage.
- **Reference data fairness:** an algorithm that uses measured velocity has more information than our position-only PLS. When testing on NGSIM, define whether the velocity field is genuinely independent or camera-derived from the same positions. Report any extra-information advantage explicitly.
- **Physical units and irregular samples:** our simple $D_3/(\Delta t)^3$ derivation assumes fixed $\Delta t$. The adaptive V-spline points toward extensions for irregular/missing data and heteroskedastic errors.
- **Modeling caution:** choosing $\gamma$ and $\lambda$ through LOOCV fits historical observations; it does not prove predictive benefit at 0.5, 1 or 2 seconds.

**Useful comparison:** test a conventional position-only cubic smoothing spline ($\gamma=0$) alongside V-spline variants where valid velocity data exist. Do not label V-spline a published nonzero-reference jerk estimator.

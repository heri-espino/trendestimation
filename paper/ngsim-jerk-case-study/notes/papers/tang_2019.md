# Tang, Yoon & Barfoot — White-noise-on-jerk prior for continuous-time trajectory estimation

**Citation:** Tim Y. Tang, David J. Yoon and Timothy D. Barfoot. *A White-Noise-On-Jerk Motion Prior for Continuous-Time Trajectory Estimation on SE(3).* [arXiv:1809.06518](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06518), original 2018 submission and 2019-era paper.

**Inspected primary material:** User-supplied **arXiv v2 source tarball**. The source file *main.tex* contains the derivation, figures, experiments and conclusions; no archival source was committed to the repository.

## Setting and model

The paper studies **continuous-time six-degree-of-freedom pose estimation** in robotics, using a Gaussian-process representation in the **SE(3)** rigid-motion group and lidar odometry. It is not a position-only finite-difference traffic-data smoother.

In the earlier STEAM Gaussian-process motion prior, **white noise on acceleration (WNOA)** yields a prior mean close to **constant velocity**. The proposed **white noise on jerk (WNOJ)** lets acceleration evolve stochastically, with prior mean favoring **constant acceleration**. States include pose, body-centric velocity and acceleration; the authors derive the SDE-based motion prior and covariance, then test the effect on lidar odometry.

The supplied source explicitly states that WNOJ is useful when there is genuine nonzero **acceleration**; a constant-velocity prior can bias a trajectory in such settings.

## Key conceptual warning

**“White noise on jerk” does not mean “constant nonzero reference jerk.”**

A WNOJ model usually centers jerk noise on **zero** and permits random fluctuations. Its mean motion is constant acceleration. Our discrete PLS with $\mu=0$ has an analogous **quadratic limiting trend** at $S=1$; our fixed $\mu\ne0$ instead centers third differences on a **nonzero** reference jerk and approaches a cubic trend. This is a meaningful distinction, but the models otherwise have different likelihoods, state spaces, priors and observation models.

## Evidence and application scope

The authors compare WNOJ against WNOA in real lidar-derived motion estimation; they report reductions in bias and odometry error across their datasets. The result is not evidence that our $d=3$ fit estimates the latent jerk of an NGSIM car more accurately.

This paper is especially useful for **theoretical motivation** of choosing derivative-order priors by physical motion order. However, it also establishes a serious alternative family for a future benchmark: a Kalman smoother or Gaussian-process model with a jerk-driven dynamic prior.

## Suggestions for Paper C

1. Position our fixed/nonzero-$\mu$ PLS as a **discrete deterministic regularizer**; compare conceptually to WNOJ as a **probabilistic continuous-time motion prior**.
2. Include zero-jerk and sustained nonzero-jerk simulation regimes: a zero-centered WNOJ prior and a nonzero-centered PLS will have different biases in those regimes.
3. If implemented, separate GP/state-space **smoothing** with access to all training samples from **filtering** or extrapolation into genuinely future time points.
4. Avoid conflating this 2018/2019 article with the separately published 2020 *A Data-Driven Motion Prior for Continuous-Time Trajectory Estimation on SE(3)*, which is a later work with a different title and contribution.

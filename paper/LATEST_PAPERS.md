## Scientific boundaries
The Springer work investigates **complete numerical selection-criterion computation** using frozen 29-case Experiment011 results. Its conditional spectral-SVD recommendation applies to repeated grids within feasible dense-memory limits, not to every series length or numerical precision problem.

The unified CSDA article focuses on the **mathematical spectral reconstruction, normalized smoothness coordinate, numerical-accuracy limitations, and time/memory complexity bounds**. Its original-algorithm provenance table distinguishes historical numerical methods from the manuscript's derived grid workload bounds. Its November 18 update explicitly decomposes the fixed difference operator D_d, compares Gram and smoother eigenanalysis with suitable conditioning cautions, distinguishes one setup from 11 new smoothness evaluations, and places both computational tables on readable native-landscape PDF pages. The latest November 18 revision directly contrasts Weinert's banded $LDL^\top$ method with SVD($D_d$), illustrates $D_3$ and $Q_3$ exactly, connects third differences to sampled jerk, and treats choosing $d$ as a separate validation problem rather than an established improvement. It disclaims universal SVD speed superiority. The PDF is a compiled draft, not a statistical validation or a journal submission.

Paper C studies **established third-difference penalized least squares** for physically interpretable discrete jerk and recorded future-position trajectories; the article explicitly reports spline-related advantages in some real-data forecasting comparisons and does not claim that PLS inherently forecasts or that independently observed real jerk is available. The Gaussian-model interpretation of S does not establish absolute sensor precision.

\title[Complete-criterion PLS selection costs]{Complete-criterion computation for penalized least-squares trend selection: a manifest-audited Windows timing study}
% Retain existing authors and institution pending explicit author verification.
\author*[1]{\fnm{Heriberto} \sur{Espino-Montelongo}}
\author[1]{\fnm{Daniela} \sur{Cort\'es-Toto}}
\affil*[1]{\orgname{Universidad de las Am\'ericas Puebla},
  \orgaddress{\city{San Andr\'es Cholula}, \state{Puebla}, \country{Mexico}}}

\abstract{Selecting a smoothing parameter for finite-difference penalized least squares requires computation of a complete statistical criterion, not merely a fitted trend. We audit implementations of ordinary cross-validation (CV), generalized cross-validation (GCV), corrected Akaike information criterion and Gaussian marginal likelihood on common normalized-smoothness candidate grids. A focused 29-case campaign has 97 additional seed trials per case on a Windows workstation. An audited six-stage cumulative report confirms 2813 scheduled case-seed jobs, including 57 nonzero process exits. Manifest-driven ingestion explicitly preserves incomplete and numerically invalid searches, resource exclusions and distinct worker-contention phases. Of 92,679 recorded or explicitly masked timing rows, 76,220 satisfy complete-search status and candidate coverage; selected-criterion agreement remains a double-precision parity check rather than independent high-precision certification. For $n=256$, order-three conditional CV, a matched banded/SVD cold-time ratio reverses from 0.70 (95\% seed-bootstrap interval 0.67--0.72) for a single candidate and series to 4.85 (4.57--5.12) for a 100-candidate search. Many larger order-four full grids fail despite successful single-candidate evaluations. These observations show that setup amortization, selection criterion, candidate mapping, workload, numerical admissibility and execution load jointly determine measured costs. No universally optimal solver or improvement in forecasting accuracy follows.}
\keywords{penalized least squares, Whittaker--Henderson smoothing, cross-validation, smoothing-parameter selection, reproducible timing, numerical reliability}

\title[Spectral smoothing-parameter selection]{Spectral decomposition for efficient smoothing-parameter selection in penalized least squares: an empirical study of complete-criterion computation}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Heriberto} \sur{Espino-Montelongo}}
\email{heriberto.espinomo@udlap.mx}
\affil*[1]{\orgname{Universidad de las Am\'ericas Puebla},
  \orgaddress{\city{San Andr\'es Cholula}, \state{Puebla}, \country{Mexico}}}

\abstract{Selecting the smoothing parameter of a finite-difference penalized least-squares estimator requires evaluation of a statistical selection criterion, not merely solution of a penalized linear system. We examine the computational advantages and numerical limitations of reusing a singular-value decomposition (SVD) across candidate parameters, compared with a structured banded-Cholesky implementation. The study covers 29 prespecified configurations, 97 random-seed realizations per configuration, multiple selection criteria and one- versus multiple-candidate workloads. Valid execution times are matched by realization, workload and execution phase, with uncertainty estimated by seed-cluster bootstrap. For an order-three conditional cross-validation problem with $n=256$, the banded/SVD complete cold-time ratio is 0.698 (95\% interval 0.671--0.724) for a single candidate but 4.846 (4.567--5.125) for a 100-candidate grid. In nine configurations, the banded complete-grid implementation fails its post-solve optimality check for all recorded grid evaluations while the spectral pipeline completes them; both methods exhibit smoothness-inversion failures in larger fourth-order configurations. Dense spectral storage nevertheless scales quadratically with series length. The results support spectral decomposition as the preferred implementation for repeated complete-criterion searches when memory permits, while retaining banded methods for isolated evaluations and memory-constrained problems.}

\keywords{penalized least squares, smoothing-parameter selection, singular-value decomposition, Whittaker--Henderson smoothing, cross-validation, numerical reliability, computational performance}

\section{Notation and roadmap of the derivations}
\label{sec:guide}
The same trend estimator can be described in three
mathematically equivalent ways. We use these descriptions
for different purposes rather than treating them as
different statistical models.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.3em,style=nextline,itemsep=2pt]
\item[$D_d$ (differences):]
The $(n-d)\times n$ matrix that converts an
$n$-point trend into its order-$d$ forward
differences. The integer $d$ is the
\emph{difference order} and $r=n-d$ is
the number of penalized directions.
\item[$Q_d=D_d^\top D_d$ (penalty):]
The symmetric $n\times n$ matrix measuring
the quadratic roughness $\|D_d\tau\|^2$.
Its $d$ zero eigenvalues correspond to
unpenalized polynomials.
\item[$A_\lambda=I+\lambda Q_d$ (normal matrix):]
The symmetric positive-definite matrix in
the linear system defining the PLS solution
for a \emph{given} penalty $\lambda$.
\item[$H_\lambda=A_\lambda^{-1}$ (smoother):]
The linear part of the map from observations
to fitted trend when the difference reference
$\mu$ is fixed; it is generally dense even
though $A_\lambda$ is banded.
\item[$U,\Sigma,V$ (SVD of $D_d$):]
A factorization of the \emph{fixed}
difference operator, with positive singular
values $\sigma_j$. The corresponding
penalty eigenvalues are $\delta_j=\sigma_j^2$.
The same basis is reusable for all $\lambda$.
\item[$S$ (normalized smoothness) and $\nu$ (degrees of freedom):]
$\nu=\tr(H_\lambda)$ measures the
smoother's effective flexibility,
while $S=(n-\nu)/(n-d)$ rescales the
loss of flexibility to the interval $[0,1]$.
\end{description}

Our logic is: formulate the PLS objective;
derive its linear system; diagonalize the
\emph{difference operator}; characterize
the smoothness coordinate; calculate
cross-validation or likelihood criteria;
then compare the cost of doing this for
many smoothing candidates. In particular,
a \emph{likelihood} introduces a stochastic
model that the deterministic PLS equations
alone do not imply. Except where stated
otherwise, the reference $\mu$ is a
\emph{prescribed number} rather than a
data-estimated parameter.

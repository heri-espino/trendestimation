\paragraph{Notation and assumptions.}
Write $n$ for the number of equally spaced observations,
$d\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$ for the difference order,
and $r=n-d$ for the penalized rank.  The observed
series is $y$, its fitted trend is $\widehat\tau$,
the prescribed order-$d$ reference is $\mu$,
and $\lambda\geq0$ is the quadratic penalty
weight.  The smoothing operator is defined directly by
$H_\lambda=(I+\lambda D_d^\top D_d)^{-1}$.
We subsequently denote its Gram penalty by
$Q_d=D_d^\top D_d$ and its \\emph{normal matrix}
by $A_\lambda=I+\lambda Q_d$, so that
$H_\lambda=A_\lambda^{-1}$.
The normal matrix is banded, whereas its inverse
is generally dense.

For the positive singular values $\sigma_j$ of
$D_d$, write $\rho_j=\sigma_j^2$;
$\nu_d(\lambda)=\tr(H_\lambda)$ is the effective
degrees of freedom and
$S_d(\lambda)=(n-\nu_d(\lambda))/r$
is the normalized trace reduction.  We first
derive the deterministic estimator, then interpret
the selected penalty under explicitly stated
stochastic assumptions.  Unless specified otherwise,
$\mu$ is a fixed numeric reference rather than
an estimate computed from the observed series.

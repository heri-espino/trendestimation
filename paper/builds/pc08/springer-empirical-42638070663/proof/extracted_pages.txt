Abstract
Selecting a smoothing parameter for finite-difference penalized least squares
requires computation of a complete statistical criterion, not merely a fitted trend.
We audit implementations of ordinary cross-validation (CV), generalized crossvalidation (GCV), corrected Akaike information criterion and Gaussian marginal
likelihood on common normalized-smoothness candidate grids. A focused 29case campaign has 97 additional seed trials per case on a Windows workstation.
An audited six-stage cumulative report confirms 2813 scheduled case-seed jobs,
including 57 nonzero process exits. Manifest-driven ingestion explicitly preserves
incomplete and numerically invalid searches, resource exclusions and distinct
worker-contention phases. Of 92,679 recorded or explicitly masked timing rows,
76,220 satisfy complete-search status and candidate coverage; selected-criterion
agreement remains a double-precision parity check rather than independent highprecision certification. For n = 256, order-three conditional CV, a matched
banded/SVD cold-time ratio reverses from 0.70 (95% seed-bootstrap interval
0.67–0.72) for a single candidate and series to 4.85 (4.57–5.12) for a 100-candidate
search. Many larger order-four full grids fail despite successful single-candidate
evaluations. These observations show that setup amortization, selection criterion, candidate mapping, workload, numerical admissibility and execution load
jointly determine measured costs. No universally optimal solver or improvement
in forecasting accuracy follows.
Keywords: penalized least squares, Whittaker–Henderson smoothing, cross-validation,
smoothing-parameter selection, reproducible timing, numerical reliability

1 Introduction
Finite-difference penalized least squares (PLS) is an established trend-smoothing
technique Whittaker (1923); Guerrero (2007). Selecting its smoothing parameter by
cross-validation (CV), generalized cross-validation (GCV), corrected Akaike information criterion (AICc), BIC, or a Gaussian marginal-likelihood criterion entails
evaluating complete statistical scores, rather than merely obtaining a fitted trend
Cortés-Toto et al. (2017). Efficient banded matrix algorithms and related Whittaker–
Henderson smoothing procedures have been developed for decades Weinert (2007,
2012); Biessy (2026); this article does not claim their invention.
We ask when a one-time dense spectral decomposition can amortize its cost over
repeated full-criterion evaluations, and when a banded procedure remains preferable.
The answer depends jointly on sample length, difference order, criterion, smoothnesscandidate count, number of series, numerical admissibility, and cache reuse. In
particular, observationwise leverage required by ordinary CV differs from the scalar
trace needed for GCV. Comparison with an explicit dense-inverse reference and a
sparse-Cholesky/banded-trace hybrid is possible only where both the full search and
its numerical checks succeed.
The principal methodological contribution of this article is a manifest-driven
audit of completed empirical results. An experiment may report every scheduled job
as recorded while retaining unsuccessful processes, partial searches and interrupted
attempts. We recover the authoritative original and continuation runs, preserve error
statuses, and estimate matched speedups with seed-cluster rather than rowwise uncertainty. This is an empirical study of complete-criterion implementation costs, separate
from the repository’s computational-complexity, high-precision numerical and NGSIM
application manuscripts.

The original, versioned Experiment006 implementation defines finite-precision AICc,
BIC and Gaussian marginal likelihood and their endpoint behavior. The observeddifference reference mean µ = Dd y is a conditional full-sample plug-in: the reported
CV is not the exact leave-one-out procedure that reestimates µ in every fold. Marginallikelihood scores from different numerical formulas may differ by a λ-independent
additive normalization; agreement of their minimizing S is checked without requiring
identical absolute score levels. Undefined criteria and partial candidate evaluations
cannot be timing winners.
Four workloads are measured: one candidate/one series, M candidates/one series,
one candidate/R series, and M candidates/R series. Both direct-S mapping and oncemapped-λ policies evaluate the same candidates S = j/M for j = 1, . . . , M , with the
endpoint S = 1 handled analytically. Single-candidate tasks use the predeclared fixed
smoothness. A valid complete-search time accounts for solver setup, smoothness-topenalty translation and every candidate’s criterion evaluation. The evaluator reports
cold and warm cost accounting; a warm total is not proof of an independently spawned
second process. At small n, both backends may pay a common spectral-mapper setup
charge.

2.3 Four complete-criterion implementation families
The original benchmark contains full spectral SVD, banded Cholesky with selectedinverse criterion diagnostics, naive explicit dense inversion, and an optional sparseCholesky plus separately charged banded diagnostics hybrid. The hybrid is not a pure
sparse Cholesky benchmark. All computations use one BLAS thread per job. Under
fixed small difference order, a banded factorization has a nominal O(nd2 ) arithmetic
3

cost, while full dense spectral decomposition incurs asymptotically greater setup time
and storage. Neither expression is measured wall time. The full search additionally
incurs criterion-dependent residual, leverage or trace calculations, mapping and cache
costs. Generalized operation counts and their proofs belong to the separate complexity
companion; this paper reports seconds only where measured.

3 Experiment011 design and canonical ingestion
3.1 Predeclared case-seed campaign
The study is the focused 29-case Experiment011 design, not the unrelated 252,000cell historical factorial plan. For each case, 97 additional seeded jobs use base seeds
109 + 106 i for i = 0, . . . , 96, stored under indices i + 3. Design case identifiers fix n, d,
criterion, amplitude, reference-mean policy, candidate count, method list, repeats, and
the four workloads. Most non-smoke cases contain two within-seed repeats and rotated
method ordering; the rotating block label is not itself an independent seed.
The final cumulative report records 2813 distinct case-seed jobs from the selfhosted Windows X64 runner PCAG103A-08. The controller used one BLAS thread per
job but changed maximum concurrent workers during the campaign: 2231 jobs were
recorded in the 24-worker phase, 52 in a one-worker phase, and 530 in a 12-worker
phase. Phase limits are not actual instantaneous CPU occupancy. These phases are
not pooled to claim isolated single-process latency, nor do summed per-method times
establish campaign throughput.

3.2 Exact run selection and numerical masks
The final report depends on six successive cumulative checkpoints. Their predecessor hashes and inherited arrays were verified, yielding 2272 canonical records from
the original run tag, then 11, 55, zero, zero and 475 additional records from successive continuations. Run tags are taken from the stage that first appended a record;
selecting every directory recursively would incorrectly treat interrupted attempts as
independent replications. The original report, source file, case and QA manifests, environment JSON and raw CSVs remain read-only. Each selected file is compared against
its SHA-256 inventory entry and a verified local backup copy.
A row is classified as a complete status-admissible search only if the process completed, its saved status is successful, its time and selected score are finite, and the
observed number of criterion evaluations equals M R. Paired methods must additionally agree on selected S within 10−10 and non-likelihood selected-score relative error
within 10−7 , using the same case, seed, repeat, workload, coordinate, executable hash,
machine environment and worker phase. Failed mappings, resource exclusions, unsuccessful processes, incomplete candidate searches and absent files remain explicitly
masked. These tests establish recorded floating-point parity, not uniform independent
high-precision correctness at large d.

3.3 Paired seed-cluster uncertainty
For paired complete cold times TA , TB > 0, the speedup of method A relative to
method B is R = TB /TA . Our summary takes the arithmetic mean of log R within
each case-seed cluster, preserving repeated method/workload outcomes together, then
exponentiates the across-seed mean. We estimate percentile 95% intervals using
700 deterministic bootstrap resamples of independent seed indices. No interval is
reported for fewer than three independently seeded clusters. The intervals quantify within-design seed variation under recorded machine conditions, not uncertainty
under different CPUs, load, OS installations, or changes in optimization software.
Conditioning on successful paired searches can introduce survivor bias.

4.3 Coordinate costs and workloads
Direct-S versus mapped-λ compares computation of the same smoothing candidates.
In the n = 256, d = 3, conditional CV, M = 100, R = 3 spectral SVD case, the
paired mapped/direct complete cold-time ratio is 0.580 (95% seed-bootstrap interval
0.570–0.590) in the 24-worker phase. This is evidence that caching the coordinate
translation is useful for that particular repeated-grid workload; it is not a universal
assertion about smoothness coordinates.
The underlying timing cell summary.csv retains medians and 10th–90th percentiles for setup, mapping, candidate evaluation, total cold time and total warm
time in each phase. The four workloads in Fig. 4 are repeated measurements within
a case-seed, not four statistically independent replications. No fit-only time is silently
substituted for complete CV or GCV search time.

5 Discussion
The principal supported result is conditional, not a global ranking: depending on
criterion and candidate count, the same banded and dense spectral implementations
reverse their timing preference. A full grid rewards amortized spectral setup, but
individual candidates can favor structured banded methods. The numerical status of
the entire grid is essential to this comparison, especially at large n and high d.
The study has four important limitations. First, this was a single Windows host
with 24-, one- and 12-worker execution phases, one BLAS thread each, and no complete
occupancy instrumentation. The phase count is a concurrency limit, not a constant
level of CPU contention, and time comparisons are not transportable estimates of

results are made.

7 Conclusions
An audited schedule of 2813 case-seed jobs supplies many useful complete-criterion
timing observations, but also demonstrates why numerical failures and incomplete
grids cannot be ignored. For supported paired cases, setup amortization, workload size
and mapping policy materially change measured costs. In particular, the n = 256, d =
3 CV case changes backend preference between a single candidate and a full grid. These
observations motivate criterion-specific and reliability-aware computational choices,
not a universally fastest implementation or a new smoothing estimator.

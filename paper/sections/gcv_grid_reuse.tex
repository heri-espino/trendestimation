\subsection{Six backends for full fits versus actual GCV grid selection}\label{sec:gcv-grid-reuse}
The preceding four-method tables concern fixed-penalty trend fitting and
scalar EDF calculations, not a complete parameter search. A separate,
reproducible companion experiment compares six numerical approaches:
fresh and cached dense solves, reusable full SVD, the general banded
solver, dense Cholesky, and \emph{banded Cholesky}. The new Cholesky
measurements use \texttt{cho\_factor}/\texttt{cho\_solve} or
\texttt{cholesky\_banded}/\texttt{cho\_solve\_banded}, respectively;
we do not retroactively relabel the general banded solver as Cholesky.
All return numerically agreeing trend fits under the same fixed
$(n,d,\mu,\lambda)$, including nonzero $\mu$.

For \emph{actual} classical GCV grid search, each solver evaluates
$\operatorname{GCV}(\lambda)=n\Vert y-\widehat\tau_\lambda\Vert_2^2/
[n-\operatorname{tr}(H_\lambda)]^2$ and selects the smallest
finite score over its supplied candidate grid. For fixed $\mu$,
$A_\lambda\widehat\tau=y+\lambda\mu D_d^\top\mathbf1$,
$A_\lambda=I+\lambda D_d^\top D_d$, and
$H_\lambda=A_\lambda^{-1}$. Consequently, changing the fixed
reference mean alters fitted values and criterion values but not
$H_\lambda$, its trace, or the coefficient matrix. For Cholesky
and dense/banded direct methods, the trace calculation requires
additional operations beyond a full trend solve. In contrast, SVD
reuses the spectral eigenvalues for EDF and the already projected
observations for the grid scores.

The \texttt{quick} reference experiment considers $d\in\{1,2,3,4\}$,
$M\in\{5,11\}$, $R\in\{1,4\}$, and fixed
$\mu\in\{0,0.15\}$ at $n=48$. It compares equally spaced
log-penalty points with uniformly spaced normalized-$S$ points
mapped to the \emph{same finite penalty endpoints}. The common
$S\to\lambda$ inversion cost is timed separately and deliberately
excluded from the method-specific numbers above. An $S$ grid and
a log-penalty grid need not contain the same interior candidates
or select the same discrete optimum; this does not contradict
continuous reparameterization invariance. Exact $S=0$ and $S=1$
are supported elsewhere in the library but excluded from the
finite-system comparison to avoid limiting/singular computational
branches.

In the recorded scenarios, reusable SVD was faster for the
\emph{end-to-end GCV selection} cases in Table~\ref{tab:gcv-six-d},
while separate full-fit-only measurements favor banded Cholesky
for many repeated reconstructions. Reusing the \emph{same} grid
across multiple independent series can amortize the preprocessing
(Table~\ref{tab:gcv-reuse}); Cholesky factors do \emph{not}
carry over across different penalty values. These are descriptive
measurements from a GitHub-hosted Linux runner with Python~3.11,
NumPy~2.4.6, SciPy~1.17.1, one BLAS thread, three repeats,
and one seeded synthetic data design, not statistical evidence
of a universally fastest backend. Raw records and environment
provenance are archived in
\texttt{paper/benchmarks/grid\_reuse\_reference/}. A separate
six-way full-fit table with its own provenance is available under
\texttt{paper/benchmarks/cholesky\_reference/}. The author's
subsequent Windows-VM \texttt{full} run is not included here
because its CSV files have not yet been archived in the repository.

The library therefore retains \emph{spectral SVD} for
\texttt{TrendSmoother} and for CV/GCV/AICc/BIC/conditional MLE
selection, while its \emph{trend-only} convenience solver defaults
to banded Cholesky. This is a workload-specific engineering choice,
not a change to the objective, selection criterion, or theory.

\subsection{A targeted three-backend engineering check}
\label{sec:three-backend-check}
The normal-equations matrix
$A_\lambda=I_n+\lambda D_d^\top D_d$ is symmetric positive
definite at finite $\lambda\geq0$ and has half-bandwidth $d$.
Consequently, direct banded Cholesky requires
$O(nd^2)$ arithmetic and $O(nd)$ storage for one factorization.
A sparse Cholesky package can exploit the same one-dimensional
sparsity, but its generic symbolic machinery need not outperform
a dedicated banded implementation. In contrast, full spectral
decomposition pays $O(n^3)$ preprocessing and $O(n^2)$ storage,
while reusable projected scalar criteria such as GCV, AICc
and residual BIC can be evaluated in $O(n)$ per interior
penalty candidate. Full fitted trends and ordinary
leave-one-out leverage calculations have additional costs.
These distinctions concern \emph{complete criterion pipelines},
not merely a single linear solve.

To check functional reproducibility, we reconciled two archived
Windows 11 numerical batches for the same
$(n,d)=(64,1)$, conditional plug-in reference mean
$\mu=\overline{D_1y}$, high-amplitude synthetic series,
residual BIC criterion, and an $M=100\,000$ target-smoothness
grid. The three complete pipelines were dense naive inversion,
reusable spectral SVD and genuine sparse CHOLMOD Cholesky followed
by separately charged banded selected-inverse diagnostics.
The normal-equations solver, smoother, and statistical
criterion were not changed. Each implementation produced
16 valid rows across two repeats, two coordinates, and four
one/multiple-candidate by one/multiple-series workloads.
In total, all 48 recorded observations had successful
numerical status. Both repeated outcomes selected $S=10^{-5}$
for the grid workloads, consistent with the known
near-interpolation behavior of the adopted residual BIC
expression. This boundary choice should not be interpreted
as evidence of a statistically well-identified interior optimum.

The maximum relative difference in selected BIC score from the
SVD reference across the same 16 workload--coordinate--repeat
keys was $2.321\times10^{-12}$ for naive inversion and
$4.463\times10^{-13}$ for the sparse-Cholesky hybrid, with
identical selected smoothness at the recorded resolution.
These \emph{float64 selected-score parity} checks are not
independent high-precision solution-error certificates.
CHOLMOD's first archived attempt had failed at dependency
initialization; the separately archived successful backfill
uses the installed scikit-sparse implementation. The short
hardware timings are not pooled with the distinct historical
80\,640-record factorial experiment.

The complete 48-row CSV, original source revisions,
worker counts, seed, workload definitions and an executable
validation script are preserved under
\texttt{paper/benchmarks/experiment010/publication\_evidence/}.
For a controlled wall-time comparison, the three methods
must be rerun under identical CPU contention, numerical
tolerances and caching conditions. Merely setting
\texttt{Workers=8} on a single method--case task does not
parallelize its factorization: the worker pool schedules
independent tasks, not partitions of one Cholesky solve.
We do not extrapolate this $n=64$ case to a universal
solver cutoff, or require a much larger brute-force grid
to establish the theory of the estimator.

\section{Measured computational results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Same statistical grid, different computational paths}
Table~\ref{tab:criteria} reproduces five \emph{measured} standalone 17-point Windows searches, using the same SVD backend at $n=48,d=2,\mu=0,R=1$. Native-$s$ mapping and one-time pretranslation score the exact same penalties. The latter avoids repeating the scalar inversion inside every scored search, although the table charges its grid conversion once to each \emph{independent} standalone task.

If the \emph{same one-time candidate translation} is shared across all five criteria, the native-$s$ total from those recorded runs was 11.234 ms, whereas grid translation paid \emph{once} plus five backend-only searches totaled 3.980 ms: a descriptive 2.823-fold reduction for this configuration. It would be incorrect to sum the standalone premapped timings as if each were a separate grid-construction expense in this shared workflow. The effect is specific to the cached application architecture and does \emph{not} establish that $s$ is intrinsically faster than $\lambda$.

\subsection{Longer series and repeated fitted curves}
The separate complete-trend benchmark, with $n$ extending to 400 and up to 256 repeated fits, documents substantial changes in winners. For $n=400,d=2,\mu=0$, the ratio of fresh dense to spectral total time was 0.158 at $M=1$ but 28.314 at $M=256$; 0.158 means the dense approach was faster for the single fit, whereas 28.314 means the spectral implementation was faster than the repeatedly fresh dense solver on the larger sweep. Against a properly general banded solver the corresponding ratios were 0.055 and 0.339, so \emph{banded remained faster} in those two recorded workloads. These are host-specific paired cases, not universal crossovers and not five-criterion selections.

The 20-seed independent solver study also finds workload-dependent differences between full SVD, symmetric eigendecomposition and banded Cholesky. Figure~\ref{fig:solver} reports the already archived geometric mean of paired SVD/banded-Cholesky total-time ratios for $d=2,\mu=0$ at three $n$ and $M$. Ratios greater than one favor Cholesky, less than one SVD. For example, at $n=96$, $M=64$, the study estimated an SVD-to-Cholesky ratio 0.959 (pointwise bootstrap interval [0.955,0.964]), while at $n=192$, $M=64$, it was 2.614 ([2.595,2.635]). These values and their uncertainty apply only to that prespecified fit-only design and processor. Pointwise intervals do not supply family-wise testing claims.

\subsection{Exact matched-grid equivalence}
The complete source audit verified 4,484 numerically valid seed-matched native-$s$/premapped-$\lambda$ grid comparisons with \emph{zero} differences in selected $s$ and scored values. This is expected from \eqref{eq:S}: a monotone coordinate transform does not change the statistical model. The computational advantage lies in \emph{the caching policy}, not statistical changes. Results from unmatched log-$\lambda$ or bounded continuous search cannot establish this equivalence because they evaluate different candidate sets.

No measured cell in this section should be considered an objective benchmark at $d=6$ or for data-derived $\widehat\mu$; those remain future research dimensions.

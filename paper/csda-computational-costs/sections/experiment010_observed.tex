\section{Completed multi-backend experiments}\label{sec:experiment010}
This section reports only the experiments completed and archived by 3 November
2026. The planned larger factorial is not presented as measured, and the
historical small-series results in Sections~\ref{sec:design}--\ref{sec:results}
are not pooled with the newer Windows timing observations.

\subsection{Full-criterion workloads}
The experiment varies series length $n$, finite-difference order $d$,
candidate smoothness count $M$, number of independent series $R$,
criterion $C$ (CV, GCV, BIC, AICc or Gaussian marginal likelihood),
reference mean $\mu$, observation amplitude, native-$S$ versus
pretranslated-$\lambda$ evaluation, and the complete numerical backend.
Three mean policies are compared:
\[
\mu=0,\qquad
\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{data}}=\frac{\mathbf1^\top D_dy}{n-d},\qquad
\mu=c=0.15.
\]
The sample-derived mean is computed separately for each observed series.
It produces conditional plug-in scores, not exact leave-one-out
re-estimation of the mean or joint marginal maximum likelihood.
For the same $(n,d)$, all three policies share the same exact map
$\Lambda_{n,d}(S)$, independent of the observations and mean policy.

All complete-criterion pipelines charge their setup, smoothness mapping,
fit, effective degrees of freedom, leverage and marginal-likelihood
diagnostics as applicable. The guarded SVD/Cholesky policy is composite;
sparse and PCG hybrid pipelines include their extra banded diagnostics.
An incomplete, unavailable or numerically invalid method is never
assigned a fictitious valid execution time.

\subsection{High-order reliability and a discovered MLE normalization error}
The independent Experiment006 85-decimal-digit fitted-trend audit
(Table~\ref{tab:experiment006-highd}) used $n=128$, $d=5,6$,
fixed $\mu=0.15$, and 28 reference problems per backend.
Under its $10^{-6}$ relative forward-error threshold, full
shift-free SVD passed 28/28, banded Cholesky 16/28, sparse LU
22/28 and FFT--PCG 14/28 accepted its convergence gate.
This is separate \emph{fit accuracy} evidence, not a high-precision
certificate for each Experiment010 criterion timing.

The original \emph{pre-correction} Experiment010 pilot reported
104 disagreements in selected criterion scores; 100 occurred
for $n=64,d=5$, data-derived $\mu$, and the profiled
Gaussian marginal-likelihood criterion. The original finite-penalty
banded, hybrid and naive benchmark implementations omitted
$\log\det(D_dD_d^\top)$ from that criterion, whereas the spectral
score and exact $S=1$ endpoint retained it. For $r=n-d$ and
positive eigenvalues $\delta_j$ of $D_d^\top D_d$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:experiment010_mle}
\begin{aligned}
&\log\det(I+\lambda D_d^\top D_d)-r\log\lambda\\
&\quad=\sum_{j=1}^r\log\left(1+\frac1{\lambda\delta_j}\right)
+\log\det(D_dD_d^\top).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Although the last term is constant among \emph{finite}
penalty candidates, omitting it destroys objective-value
comparability with the correctly normalized exact
$S=1$ candidate. The implemented correction evaluates
this term without a dense determinant using the identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:experiment010_gram}
\det(D_dD_d^\top)=
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}
\frac{\binom{n+j}{2j+1}}{\binom{2j}{j}}.
\end{equation}
After revising the MLE benchmarks and regression tests,
the repeated pilot had only 16 score disagreements, all
from the high-order naive inverse. The remaining discrepancies
and forward-error risks are not dismissed by score parity.
Old and corrected raw results are preserved on distinct
immutable GitHub branches. Correcting a statistical
criterion is a scientific finding, not a claimed speedup.

\subsection{Conditional computational minimum: a testable hypothesis}
For a future workload $w$, define the set of eligible
complete pipelines
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F(w)=
\{b:\text{$b$ installed, memory-feasible, criterion-complete,
and independently accuracy-certified}\}.
\end{equation}
An operational hypothesis is to estimate the machine-specific
full execution time $\widehat T_b(w)$---including setup,
$S\mapsto\lambda$ mapping, factorization, criterion
diagnostics and reuse---then choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:experiment010_selector}
\widehat b(w)\in\arg\min_{b\in\mathcal F(w)}\widehat T_b(w).
\end{equation}
On failure, a future dispatcher would try the next
valid, feasible candidate and record extra time.
The existing results do \emph{not} establish a calibrated
ten-method model, independent class-balanced held-out
prediction of crossovers, or measured fallback regret.
Leading-order arithmetic alone cannot predict actual
execution time without constants, conditioning,
iterative-solver convergence and memory constraints.
Thus~\eqref{eq:experiment010_selector} is a \emph{research
proposal}, not a deployed, empirically proven selector.

\subsection{Reproducibility and precise limits}
The corrected-source Actions runs are
\texttt{41587467639} (smoke and initial pilot),
\texttt{41587723422} (pilot continuations) and
\texttt{41642508020} (targeted $n=256,512$ frontier).
The pre-correction failure-discovery run is
\texttt{41586066141}. Each individual result branch
and precise case path is indexed by
\texttt{paper/csda-computational-costs/OBSERVED\_RESULTS\_SNAPSHOT.md}
and \texttt{paper/benchmarks/experiment010/RESULTS\_INDEX.md}.
The full original CSVs, including failures and no-reference
statuses, are the data source rather than any plotted
or theoretical extrapolation. No execution at
$n=16384$ or $131072$ and no completion of a
10,000--100,000-point search is inferred from the
smaller archived batches. Numerical certification,
large-series feasibility and the general conditional
winner rule remain separate research questions.

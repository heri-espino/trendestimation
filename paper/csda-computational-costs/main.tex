\begin{abstract}
Finite-difference penalized least-squares (PLS) trends can be parameterized by a normalized, interpretable smoothness index $s\in[0,1]$ rather than the scale-dependent penalty $\lambda$. Efficient use of this coordinate requires more than a fast linear solver: exact smoothness inversion, conditional effective degrees of freedom, leave-one-out leverage values, criterion evaluation and repeated-grid reuse can dominate complete computational time. We present an algorithmic analysis for difference orders $d=1,\ldots,6$ and fixed zero or nonzero means of penalized differences, including an exact banded selected-inverse formulation for full conditional CV, GCV, AICc, residual BIC and Gaussian marginal-likelihood evaluation. We define four crossed workloads: one or many smoothness candidates evaluated for one or many independent series. Nine full-criterion pipelines---naive dense inversion, spectral SVD, banded Cholesky and six explicitly costed banded/sparse/iterative hybrids---form a staged, resource-gated evaluation design over seven sample sizes through $131072$, four positive smoothness grids through $100000$ candidates, two observation magnitudes and fixed reference means. This large-scale factorial is a \emph{planned experiment, not an empirical result}. The existing two-seed, $80640$-row Windows timing corpus supplies a historical baseline: sharing one exactly matched smoothness-grid translation across five criteria reduced measured elapsed time by factors of $2.67$ at $d=3$ and $2.82$ at $d=2$ for specified $n=48$ workflows. We distinguish these host-specific observations from theoretical complexity and future results, require numerical validity before timing comparisons and preserve $s=1$ as an exact constrained endpoint.
\end{abstract}

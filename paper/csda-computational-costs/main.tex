\begin{abstract}
Finite-difference penalized least-squares (PLS) trends may be indexed by
normalized smoothness $S\in[0,1]$ rather than a scale-dependent penalty
$\lambda$. Selecting an efficient implementation requires more than
one linear solve: the complete CV, GCV, AICc, BIC and marginal-likelihood
workloads include grid inversion, factorization, exact trace or leverage
diagnostics and setup reuse. We compare ten existing complete-criterion
pipeline policies, including explicit dense inversion, full spectral SVD,
banded Cholesky and banded, sparse or iterative hybrids. The completed,
corrected-source Windows runs archive 5570 successful raw timing
observations across 450 method--case attempts; 1102 additional
workload records failed or were partial, 18 disagreed with a reference
criterion score, and 72 lacked a valid spectral comparator.
For the matched $n=256,d=1$, GCV workload at fixed $\mu=0$,
banded Cholesky took 8.13\,ms versus 13.80\,ms for SVD with one
smoothness candidate, whereas spectral SVD took 26.68\,ms versus
61.31\,ms for Cholesky with 100 candidates (two timing repetitions
on a single Windows runner). The same descriptive winner reversal
appeared under the empirical-difference plug-in mean and fixed
$\mu=0.15$. An MLE-normalization discrepancy discovered in the
initial pilot was corrected analytically, reducing recorded
score disagreements from 104 to 16. We distinguish fitted-trend
accuracy from criterion-score parity, preserve unavailable and
unmeasured cases without fictitious times, and give a conditional
fastest-feasible-method rule as an \emph{unvalidated research
hypothesis}, not a deployed dispatcher or an exhaustive factorial
conclusion. All source revisions and raw outputs remain archived.
\end{abstract}

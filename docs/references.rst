Scientific references and methodological boundaries
===================================================

For a mathematical synthesis and reproducible optimization examples, see
:doc:`smoothness_optimization`.

Established statistical methodology
-----------------------------------

* Guerrero, V. M. (2007). *Time series smoothing by penalized least
  squares*. Statistics & Probability Letters, 77, 1225–1234.
  DOI: `10.1016/j.spl.2007.03.006`.
* Cortés-Toto, D., Guerrero, V. M., and Reyes, H. J. (2017).
  *Trend smoothness achieved by penalized least squares with the
  smoothing parameter chosen by optimality criteria*.
  Communications in Statistics — Simulation and Computation,
  46(2), 1492–1507. DOI: `10.1080/03610918.2015.1005236`.
* Weinert, H. L. (2007). *Efficient computation for
  Whittaker–Henderson smoothing*. Computational Statistics & Data
  Analysis, 52, 959–974. DOI: `10.1016/j.csda.2006.11.038`.
* Biessy, G. (2026). *Whittaker–Henderson smoothing revisited:
  A modern statistical framework for practical use*. ASTIN Bulletin, 56.
  DOI: `10.1017/asb.2025.10061`.

Non-claims
----------

Diagonalization, arbitrary admissible differencing orders,
classical selection criteria, and the nonzero reference level of
differences are **previously published mathematical ideas**.

This distribution deliberately excludes our experimental
forecast-horizon-based CV, selected predictive trends, and
temporally matched local-minimum branches. An in-sample smoother
must not be interpreted as an ex ante forecast.

The notebooks include deterministic *synthetic* examples.
They do not reproduce the published papers' empirical records
or establish statistical superiority on held-out future outcomes.
